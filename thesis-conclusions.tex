\chapter{Conclusions} \label{chapConclusions}

This thesis has addressed several key challenges in realizing efficient bio-inspired locomotion on real aquatic robots. While prior work has produced a wide range of biomimetic swimming hardware, the joint design of morphology, compliance, dynamics, and onboard sensing has remained fragmented. We have argued that aquatic locomotion is best engineered as a problem of embodied multiphysics, where the body, compliant structures, actuators, sensors, and surrounding fluid form a single coupled dynamical system, and that meaningful progress requires modeling this coupling explicitly rather than as an afterthought. Building on this view, the thesis developed coherent contributions across embodied design, variational modeling, optimization-based locomotion, and distributed onboard sensing.

Central to the thesis is the programmable online stiffness modulation (POSM) framework, which converts joint stiffness from a fixed mechanical property into a within-cycle control variable. We developed a modular multi-joint mechanism whose sliding-laminate flexures provide time-varying, joint-specific compliance with simple two-channel actuation, alongside a variational-mechanics model with quasi-steady hydrodynamic forces that supports gradient-based trajectory optimization over both motion and stiffness schedules. Hardware experiments on a fixed-base testbed and on an untethered fish-inspired robot capable of both body-caudal-fin (BCF) undulation and drag-based pectoral-fin paddling showed that programmable joint-specific stiffness yields up to 89\% higher thrust in drag-based swimming, up to 19\% higher mean thrust in BCF, and up to 34.8\% reduction in cost of transport over static and spatially uniform baselines.

We then extended this modeling perspective from reduced-order hydrodynamics toward strongly coupled fluid--robot multiphysics, contributing to a unified, differentiable simulator that treats the no-slip interface as an integral constraint and enables gait optimization for both sustained undulation and aggressive transient maneuvers such as C-start escapes. To evaluate these modeling and locomotion methods in real water, we developed two complementary mechatronic platforms: a modular undulatory swimming robot for steady and transient experiments, and the low-cost, open-source SwimGym drag-based system together with its digital-twin simulator. We further broadened the embodied-sensing component from articulated swimmers to modular continuum robots, introducing a reconfigurable platform with 3D-printed compliant joints of finite-element-characterized stiffness and a self-contained pose-estimation pipeline that maps onboard magnetic measurements to inter-segment orientations, demonstrating that distributed local sensing can replace external tracking infrastructure across traversal, grasping, and large-deformation manipulation tasks. Complementing these structural and sensing contributions, we examined printable thermoelectric ink as a candidate material technology, extending the embodied-design theme toward soft, multifunctional substrates for thermally responsive robotic devices.

Taken together, the work in this thesis demonstrates that programmable compliance, variational modeling, embodied platform design, and distributed onboard sensing form a coherent design space for bio-inspired aquatic robots, one in which morphology, dynamics, and sensing are jointly exploited rather than designed in isolation. Looking ahead, the most natural extensions are closed-loop control of POSM under unsteady inflow and obstacle interaction, integration of strongly coupled fluid--robot multiphysics into stiffness optimization, and the use of distributed local flow and proprioceptive sensing to support fully autonomous bio-inspired swimming outside controlled laboratory tanks.
